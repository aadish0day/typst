## 3.1.1 Statement of the Problem

WhatsApp is India's dominant private-messaging platform, and forwarded messages — text, images, and short clips passed peer-to-peer between family, neighbourhood, and community groups — are its single largest vector for the spread of misinformation. Because WhatsApp is end-to-end encrypted and forwarding happens inside closed group chats rather than on the open web, a false claim (a fabricated health cure, a doctored political quote, a fake government scheme, a communal rumour) can reach millions of readers within hours while remaining completely invisible to search-engine indexing, public fact-checking websites, and platform-level content moderation. By the time a claim is noticed and formally debunked, it has typically already completed several generations of forwarding.

**The problem, stated formally:** there is no fast, low-friction, source-accountable mechanism by which an ordinary WhatsApp user who receives a suspicious forward can (a) check whether it has already been verified, (b) get it verified quickly if it has not, and (c) obtain a verified counter-artifact in the *same format and medium* (a shareable image, not a web link) that can travel back through the group chats where the original claim spread.

## 3.1.2 Why Existing Fact-Checking Approaches Fail

The two categories of fact-checking effort that currently exist around WhatsApp forwards each fail for structurally different reasons, and neither is adequate on its own:

### A. Institutional Fact-Checkers

Organizations such as PIB Fact Check, established newsrooms, and dedicated misinformation-focused NGOs are professionally rigorous but operationally centralized:

- **Latency mismatch.** A viral forward can complete its most damaging spread cycle within a few hours; a professional investigative debunk typically takes hours to multiple days to research, write, and publish.
- **Staffing bottleneck.** A small, fixed editorial team cannot scale linearly with the volume of daily forwards across health, political, financial, and religious categories, particularly regional-language and hyper-local rumours that never reach national editorial attention at all.
- **Format mismatch.** Institutional debunks are published as long-form web articles. A WhatsApp user who has just received a punchy, alarming forward is unlikely to read (let alone re-forward) a 1,000-word article; the correction rarely travels back through the same channel the misinformation used.
- **Discoverability gap.** Because the original claim was never public (it lived inside a private, encrypted group), the eventual public debunk is not automatically connected to it — the burden of *finding* the correction falls entirely on a reader who has to think to search for it.

### B. Ad-Hoc Community Debunking

The informal alternative — a group member replying "this is fake" underneath the forward — is fast but structurally unaccountable:

- **No persistence.** The correction exists only as one message inside one group's chat history; it is invisible to every other group the same forward is circulating in simultaneously.
- **No source discipline.** A reply of "this is fake" carries no citation, no reasoning, and no way for another reader to independently assess whether the claim is credible.
- **No reputation accountability.** Anyone can assert anything with equal authority; there is no track record distinguishing a member who is reliably right from one who is not.
- **No duplicate recognition.** The same claim resurfacing under slightly reworded phrasing in a different group is treated as a brand-new, unverified rumour every time, because there is no shared record across groups.

## 3.1.3 How FactStamp's Approach Differs

FactStamp is defined as the problem-solving system that sits between these two failure modes — retaining the *speed and reach* of ad-hoc community debunking while imposing the *source discipline and auditability* of institutional fact-checking, and specifically re-engineered for the WhatsApp-forward medium rather than the open web:

1. **A single shared record, not per-group replies.** Every claim submitted (as text or an OCR-extracted screenshot) is checked against a persistent corpus using Jaccard token-overlap similarity (`J(A,B) = |A∩B| / |A∪B|`, threshold ≥ 0.75, `src/lib/duplicateDetection.ts`). A claim resurfacing in a different group under different wording is recognised and instantly routed to its existing verdict instead of being treated as new.
2. **Distributed verification instead of a single editorial gate.** No individual moderator or institution unilaterally decides a verdict. A minimum quorum of three independent, authenticated community verifiers must each cite a real source and cast a verdict before consensus is computed (`ClaimsContext.tsx`, `verificationCount >= 3`).
3. **Evidence-weighted consensus, not a headcount vote.** The final confidence score is a function of verifier agreement, verifier reputation history, and the credibility tier of cited sources (`Confidence = 0.40×AgreementRatio + 0.30×AvgVerifierReputation + 0.30×SourceQualityScore`, `src/lib/confidenceScore.ts`) — a claim "verified" by three low-credibility sources scores lower than one verified by high-authority domains (WHO, PIB, RBI).
4. **A time-bounded resolution guarantee.** Claims that fail to reach quorum consensus within `CONSENSUS_DEADLINE_DAYS = 7` auto-settle as `CONTESTED` rather than sitting indefinitely unresolved, avoiding the institutional failure mode of claims simply never being addressed.
5. **A counter-artifact in the native medium of the misinformation.** Instead of a web article, FactStamp exports a shareable PNG fact-check card 1080 px wide, with content-dependent height (`html-to-image`, `src/components/FactCheckCard.tsx`) — the same visual, forwardable format WhatsApp misinformation itself uses — so a verified correction can travel back through the exact forward chains the original claim used.
6. **A zero-friction submission path for non-technical users.** A user can submit a screenshot of the forward exactly as received; 100%-client-side OCR (Tesseract.js WebAssembly, `src/services/ocrService.ts`) extracts the claim text and strips WhatsApp chat chrome (timestamps, checkmarks, carrier/battery bars) automatically, with no server-side image upload required.

## 3.1.4 Scope Boundary of the Problem Addressed

FactStamp is deliberately scoped to solve the *verification and re-sharing* problem, not to intervene inside WhatsApp itself. It does not hook into WhatsApp's proprietary client or messaging protocol, does not perform automated AI/LLM-based truth judgments in place of human verifiers, and does not claim to prevent a claim from being forwarded in the first place. The problem it solves is narrower and more tractable: *once a claim exists, make it fast, cheap, and auditable to check whether it is true — and make the resulting verdict as forwardable as the misinformation it corrects.*
