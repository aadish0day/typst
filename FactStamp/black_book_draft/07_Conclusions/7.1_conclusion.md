# 7.1 Conclusion

FactStamp set out to close a specific gap identified in Chapter 1: WhatsApp forwards spread misinformation faster than either institutional fact-checkers or unstructured group-chat debunking can respond to it. What has been delivered is not a prototype or a design exercise but a fully functional, deployable web application — every claim made in this report is traceable to a real file in the source tree at `/home/aadish/Documents/Github/FactStamp`. Revisiting the eight objectives set out in Section 1.2, each has been substantively achieved:

1. **Reduce the time-to-debunk.** Any user can submit a claim directly through the web app rather than waiting for an institutional fact-checker to notice a forward — achieved by the multimodal submission pipeline (text or screenshot) that routes straight into the verification queue.
2. **Eliminate redundant verification effort.** Achieved through the Jaccard token-overlap duplicate detector (`src/lib/duplicateDetection.ts`, threshold ≥ 0.75), which recognizes a resubmitted claim under slightly different wording and returns the existing verdict instead of re-queuing it.
3. **Replace single-source trust with a distributed quorum.** Achieved: no single verifier or moderator can settle a verdict alone — `src/contexts/ClaimsContext.tsx` and `src/services/firebaseService.ts` each enforce a minimum of three verifications before a claim can resolve, while `firestore.rules` independently re-validates every verification write server-side (Case C of the `claims/{claimId}` update rule) so that no verification can be forged or replaced by a client.
4. **Weight consensus by evidence quality, not headcount.** Achieved via the weighted confidence engine in `src/lib/confidenceScore.ts` — `Confidence = 0.40×Agreement + 0.30×Reputation + 0.30×SourceQuality` — so a unanimous vote from low-reputation verifiers citing weak sources scores lower than the same unanimous vote backed by trusted verifiers and authoritative citations.
5. **Make verified facts shareable in the native medium of the misinformation.** Achieved: the `html-to-image`-based card generator (`src/components/FactCheckCard.tsx`) exports a 1080px-wide PNG (a 540px-wide card rasterized at a 2× pixel ratio, its height growing with the claim text and source list) sized for WhatsApp, carrying the verdict, confidence score, and cited sources back into the same forward chains the original claim traveled through.
6. **Lower the barrier to submission for non-technical users.** Partially achieved — client-side OCR (`src/services/ocrService.ts`, Tesseract.js) extracts text from an uploaded screenshot and strips WhatsApp chat chrome automatically, but this only works for English-script forwards (see Section 7.2).
7. **Build verifier accountability over time.** Achieved through the reputation system that raises or lowers a verifier's score based on alignment with eventual consensus, feeding directly back into the confidence-score weighting of every claim that verifier participates in.
8. **Give the public visibility into misinformation trends.** Achieved via the Recharts-powered analytics dashboard (`src/pages/Dashboard.tsx`, `src/lib/weeklyReport.ts`), surfacing category volume and rolling weekly "most-debunked" reporting.

Objective 6 is the only one delivered with a caveat rather than a clean achievement, and that caveat — English-only OCR — is treated honestly in Section 7.2 rather than glossed over.

### 7.1.1 Significance of the System

FactStamp's significance is not that it introduces a novel cryptographic primitive or a breakthrough machine-learning model — it deliberately avoids AI-generated verdicts entirely, in favor of human, source-cited judgment. Its significance is architectural and social: it occupies a middle ground that neither of the two dominant fact-checking approaches covers.

Institutional fact-checkers (PIB Fact Check, AltNews, BOOM Live) are authoritative but structurally slow — a small professional staff cannot scale to the volume of daily WhatsApp forwards across health, political, financial, and religious categories, and their long-form article format does not compete visually with an incendiary forward inside a chat thread. Ad-hoc group-chat debunking — someone replying "this is fake" — is fast but leaves no durable record, no source discipline, and no way to recognize the same claim the next time it resurfaces under different wording.

FactStamp sits between these two failure modes by being simultaneously:

- **Decentralized** — verification authority is distributed across a community quorum rather than concentrated in an editorial desk, so throughput scales with the number of active verifiers rather than being capped by a fixed newsroom headcount.
- **Zero-infrastructure-cost** — the entire platform runs on Firebase's free Spark tier plus static hosting, with no subscription server, no paid vision API, and no advertising-funded revenue model required to keep it running; the operating cost structure that makes institutional fact-checking hard to scale simply does not apply here.
- **Privacy-preserving** — OCR runs entirely client-side via WebAssembly; a WhatsApp screenshot, which often contains other participants' names and phone numbers in its chat header, is never transmitted to a third-party cloud vision API. Verification stays on-device until the user chooses to submit only the extracted text.
- **Source-transparent** — every verdict is backed by a visible, auditable chain of cited URLs and a mathematically defined confidence formula, in contrast to both the opaque internal review process of a newsroom and the zero-citation nature of a group-chat reply.

Taken together, FactStamp demonstrates that a scientifically defensible, quorum-governed verification process can be built and operated at genuinely zero infrastructure cost, without surrendering the human judgment and citation discipline that make a fact-check trustworthy in the first place. That is the project's core contribution as a BSc Information Technology capstone: not a single novel algorithm, but a coherent, fully working system that closes a real, named gap between two existing and inadequate alternatives.
